# Free Bookmark Alternatives

If a big-name bookmark manager is too expensive, too intrusive, or too complicated, you have plenty of genuinely free - and in some cases open source - alternatives. Here's what's worth your time in 2026.

## The honest summary

Every bookmark tool claims to be "free," but almost all of them make you trade something: your email, your reading habits, or your link list as data. The tools below avoid most of that.

## 1. SaveBuddy - free and open source

The closest thing to a genuinely free, private bookmark tool:

- **No account** - install and start saving links immediately.
- **No cloud** - links stay in your browser's `localStorage`.
- **No tracking** - no analytics, no telemetry, no ad profiles.
- **Open source (MIT)** - full transparency; you can read every line of code.
- One-click save tab, manual URL saves, edit, copy, delete, favicons, and an `Alt+S` shortcut.

## 2. Chrome's built-in bookmarks

Still underrated. Modern Chrome bookmarks include folders, search, drag-and-drop, and cross-device sync. For basic needs it costs nothing extra and requires no new tool. The limits: the bookmarks bar gets cluttered fast, and everything you save is linked to your Google account.

## 3. Raindrop.io free tier

Raindrop's free plan is genuinely generous: 100 collections and 100,000 bookmarks. It adds screenshots, tags, and cloud sync. In exchange, your bookmarks live on their servers and the free version nudges you toward the $28/year plan for full-text search.

## 4. Pocket free tier

Pocket's free tier includes unlimited saves, tags, and the reading view - a proper free product for the "read later" crowd. Your reading queue is stored on Pocket's servers and feeds their recommendation algorithm. If you're privacy-conscious, that matters.

## 5. Diigo free tier

Diigo bundles bookmarking with highlighting and sticky notes - great for research, with things like shared group lists. The interface and web app are dated, and the free tier limits like "no full-text search" push you toward paid plans.

## What about open source specifically?

Open source matters if you want to verify what a tool does with your data. In the bookmark space, genuinely open source tools are rare - SaveBuddy is one, which is exactly how it earns the "no tracking" claim: the [source code](https://github.com/gautamkaran/SaveBuddy) is on GitHub.

## How to choose a free tool

1. **Decide on sync first.** Need links on every device? Cloud tool. Fine with one machine? Local-first.
2. **Decide what data you'll pay with.** An account and your bookmark history are a cost - don't ignore it.
3. **Start minimal.** You can always upgrade to a heavier tool. Going the other way is painful.

## Recommendation

For most people, the best combo in 2026 is:

- **SaveBuddy** for quick, private, local link keeping on your main machine.
- **Native Chrome bookmarks** for anything you want synced across devices.
- Pocket **only if** you specifically want an offline reading queue.

Everything else is paying with your data for features you'll probably never use.
