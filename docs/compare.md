# SaveBuddy vs Alternatives

SaveBuddy takes a deliberately different approach to link saving: no account, no cloud, no tracking - just a fast, private list of links stored locally in your browser.

Here's how it stacks up against the most popular alternatives.

## At a glance

| Feature              | SaveBuddy      | Raindrop.io    | Pocket         | Evernote Clipper |
| -------------------- | -------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ---------------- |
| Price                | Free (MIT)     | Freemium       | Freemium       | Freemium         |
| Account required     | No             | Yes            | Yes            | Yes              |
| Cloud sync           | No             | Yes            | Yes            | Yes              |
| Data stays local     | Yes            | No             | No             | No               |
| Open source          | Yes            | No             | No             | No               |
| Keyboard shortcut    | Yes (`Alt+S`)  | Yes            | Yes            | Yes              |
| Reading view         | No             | Limited        | Excellent      | Yes              |
| Collections/folders  | No             | Yes            | Tags only      | Notebooks        |
| Best for             | Fast, private saves | Organizing a library | Reading later | Note-taking |

## Where SaveBuddy wins

**No account.** You click, it saves, done. There's nothing to sign up for and nothing to remember a password for.

**Full privacy.** Your link list never leaves your computer. No analytics, no tracking, no server-side "reading progress" profiles. For sensitive research or personal browsing, that's a real advantage.

**Speed and simplicity.** A single permission (`tabs`), a tiny popup, and a keyboard shortcut. Opening, saving, and finding links takes less than a second.

**Offline by nature.** Because links live in `localStorage`, the popup works even when you're not connected.

## Where the alternatives win

**Cross-device sync.** Raindrop and Pocket follow you across phones, tablets, and computers. SaveBuddy keeps links on one machine.

**Richer features.** Raindrop offers collections, tags, screenshots, and full-text search (paid). Pocket has the best reading view in the category. Evernote clips entire pages into notebooks.

**Large-scale libraries.** If you maintain thousands of organized bookmarks, a cloud manager with search is the practical choice.

## Detailed comparisons

### SaveBuddy vs Raindrop.io
- Raindrop is the organizing power tool: folders, screenshots, and search, with a generous free tier (100 collections).
- SaveBuddy is the zero-friction local keeper: install, save, done.
- [Full analysis](/blog/bookmark-manager-comparison/)

### SaveBuddy vs Pocket
- Pocket is built for "read later": clean offline reading view, tags, and a great mobile app.
- SaveBuddy is built for "keep this": fast local saves with no reading ecosystem attached.
- Your choice depends on your workflow: reading queue vs. link keeper.

### SaveBuddy vs Evernote Web Clipper
- Evernote clips the entire page into a note-taking system - powerful, but subscription-heavy and tied to their cloud.
- SaveBuddy saves just the link, locally, free forever.

### SaveBuddy vs Chrome's built-in bookmarks
- Built-in bookmarks sync across devices and have folders.
- SaveBuddy offers a cleaner popup with favicons, in-place editing, one-click save-tab, and a keyboard shortcut.
- Many users run both: native bookmarks for sync, SaveBuddy for quick private keeps.

## Can you use both?

Yes - and it's actually the recommended setup for many people. Use SaveBuddy (or native bookmarks) for quick private saves on your main machine, and a cloud tool like Pocket for the articles you plan to read on your phone.
