# The 10 Best Chrome Extensions to Save Links in 2026

Saving links is something every Chrome user does, but most people never find a tool that fits the way they actually work. Built-in bookmarks get buried, folders turn into a mess, and browser sync creates duplicates. The right extension fixes all of that.

Here are the 10 best Chrome extensions to save links in 2026, covering free tools, local-first options, and full bookmark managers.

## How we picked these

Every extension below was judged on five things:

- Does it save links fast?
- Can you find links again later?
- Is it free or worth the price?
- How much does it interrupt your workflow?
- Where does your data live?

## 1. SaveBuddy

**Best for: people who want simplicity, privacy, and no account.**

[SaveBuddy](/) is a Manifest V3 Chrome extension that saves links from a small popup - no account, no cloud, no tracking. Everything lives in your browser's local storage.

- Save any URL, or save the current tab in one click.
- Edit, copy, and delete links from the popup.
- Favicons next to every link so you can scan them fast.
- Keyboard shortcut (`Alt+S`) to open the popup.
- Completely free and [open source](https://github.com/gautamkaran/SaveBuddy).

It's the right tool when you want a lightweight link keeper with zero sign-up friction and full privacy.

## 2. Raindrop.io

**Best for: organized collections and cross-device access.**

Raindrop is a full bookmark manager with collections, tags, and cloud sync. The free plan covers 100 collections and 100,000 bookmarks. It's powerful but heavier - you trade simplicity for features like full-text search and automatic metadata extraction.

## 3. Pocket

**Best for: reading an article later.**

Pocket is built around the "save for later" reading flow. One click saves a page, and the reading view strips out ads and clutter. It's less about organizing bookmarks and more about a clean offline reading experience. The free tier is generous.

## 4. Evernote Web Clipper

**Best for: saving full pages and note-takers.**

If you already live in Evernote, the web clipper is a no-brainer. It saves complete pages to your notebooks with annotations. If you don't use Evernote, it pulls you into a subscription-heavy ecosystem.

## 5. Instapaper

**Best for: long-form reading queues.**

Instapaper is Pocket's classic rival - fast saving, offline reading, and a bit more editorial in how it handles text. A subscription unlocks unlimited highlights and notes.

## 6. Diigo

**Best for: researchers and students.**

Diigo is a bookmarking tool with highlighting and sticky notes baked in. You can tag research, annotate pages, and share lists. It's powerful for coursework, but the interface shows its age.

## 7. SugarSync Files

**Best for: bookmarking large file collections.**

SugarSync focuses on documents and files in the browser rather than quick URL saving. It's aimed more at cloud storage users than link collectors.

## 8. Too many tabs

**Best for: people drowning in open tabs.**

If you hoard tabs instead of bookmarks, Too many tabs turns your tab overflow into a searchable list you save from and restore later. It rewards a specific workflow: long research sessions with dozens of tabs open.

## 9. Bookmark Manager (by GWeb)

**Best for: minimal single-purpose bookmarking.**

A simple, no-frills bookmark manager that sits in your browser and keeps links organized with folders. Nothing fancy, nothing cloud-based.

## 10. The built-in Chrome bookmarks

Worth remembering: Chrome's own bookmarks bar improved a lot in recent versions. You get folders, search in the bookmark manager, and cross-device sync. If your needs are basic and you don't mind a cluttered bar, it genuinely works.

## The verdict

If you just want to **save links fast with no account and no cloud**, SaveBuddy is the easiest option - you install it, hit `Alt+S`, and you're done. If you need **organized collections across all your devices**, Raindrop or Pocket is a better fit.

The main thing to avoid: signing up for a bloated "free" tool that sells you storage you'll never use. Match the tool to how you actually work.
