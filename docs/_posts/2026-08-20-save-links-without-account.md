# How to Save and Organize Links Without an Account

Almost every bookmarking tool on the market wants something from you before you can save your first link: an email, a password, a cloud account, or worse - permission to track what you save.

But you don't need any of that. It's completely possible to save and organize links locally, with no account, no sign-up, and no data leaving your machine. This guide shows you how - and why it matters.

## Why a "no account" bookmark tool is worth it

### 1. Your data stays yours

When links live in your browser's local storage, the list of sites you save never becomes a dataset for someone else's ad targeting. For anything sensitive - research, medical pages, job searches - that privacy is a real benefit.

### 2. Zero sign-up friction

The fastest way to save a link is a tool you can click and use immediately. Every account you create on the way is friction you don't need.

### 3. No subscription creep

Local-first tools don't need a cloud to work, so they have less reason to charge you monthly. Plenty of them are free, one-time, or open source.

### 4. It works offline

No account usually means no server requirement. Your links keep working even when you're offline.

## How local-first link saving works

Local-first extensions store your links in your browser's own storage (usually `localStorage` or IndexedDB). When you open the popup, it reads from your device and renders the list.

That means:

- Opening and searching your links is instant.
- Deleting is immediate and permanent - no "but it's synced everywhere" surprises.
- Uninstalling the extension removes the data with it.

## The trade-off: no sync across devices

The honest trade-off is that a local-first tool keeps links on one computer. If you split your time between a desktop and a laptop, your saved links won't follow you automatically.

If cross-device sync is a hard requirement, you need a cloud tool. If you mostly work on one machine - or you're willing to export your links occasionally - local-first is a great fit.

## The best no-account way to save links

[SaveBuddy](/) is a free, open source Chrome extension designed around exactly this idea:

- **Save the current tab** in one click, or **type a URL** manually.
- **Edit** titles and URLs, **copy** links, and **delete** them anytime.
- Everything stays in your browser's `localStorage` - no account, no cloud, no analytics, no tracking.
- Keyboard shortcut (`Alt+S`) to open the popup instantly.
- A single minimal permission: `tabs`, used only for "save current tab".

Install it in about 30 seconds - see the [installation guide](/installation/).

## Other local-first options

- **Chrome's built-in bookmarks** - no account, works offline. The bookmark manager got a big facelift and is worth a second look.
- **Just the Docs-style personal sites** - some people keep a personal "links" page in a static site they control. More effort, but fully yours.

## How to keep things organized without folders

Local-first tools tend to be flat lists, so structure comes from how you use them:

- **Prefix titles** with a category, like `Dev -`, `Recipes -`, `Watch -` to scan quickly.
- **Delete aggressively** - a link you never revisit is noise.
- **Export backups** periodically if the tool supports it, so a reinstall never loses your list.
