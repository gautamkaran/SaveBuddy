# Best Free Bookmark Manager: Raindrop vs Pocket vs SaveBuddy

Pick any "best bookmark manager" list and three names keep coming up: Raindrop.io, Pocket, and whatever the list author found that week. The truth is that these tools serve very different workflows, and "best" depends on what you're actually trying to do.

This comparison breaks down the three strongest options of 2026 - plus SaveBuddy, the newcomer that takes a different approach entirely.

## The three approaches to bookmarking

Before comparing tools, understand the three philosophies:

1. **The organizer** (Raindrop.io) - collections, tags, search, screenshots, archives. Built for people who want a library.
2. **The reader** (Pocket) - "save for later" reading queues with a clean reading view. Built for people who consume articles.
3. **The keeper** (SaveBuddy) - a fast, private list of links you actually want back. Built for people who just want links, fast.

## Raindrop.io

**The organizer.**

Raindrop saves any page as a "bookmark" with automatic metadata: the title, description, and a screenshot of the page. Assets are saved in the cloud, so your collection is consistent everywhere.

- **Free plan:** 100 collections, 100,000 bookmarks - very generous.
- **Paid plan:** ~$28/year adds full-text search, duplicate detection, and more.
- **Sync:** Yes, across all devices.
- **Data:** stored on Raindrop's servers.
- **Best for:** people building a searchable personal library with lots of organization.

## Pocket

**The reader.**

Pocket focuses on one flow: save a page, read it later in a stripped-down view. It's excellent for "I want to read this but not right now."

- **Free plan:** unlimited saves, tags, and the reading view.
- **Paid plan:** ~$4.99/month adds permanent library, full-text search, and suggested tags.
- **Sync:** Yes.
- **Data:** stored on Pocket's servers.
- **Best for:** article hoarders who mainly want a clean reading experience.

## SaveBuddy

**The keeper.**

SaveBuddy does one thing and does it fast: it keeps links local. There's no account, no cloud, no analytics - just a popup that saves, edits, copies, and deletes your links right in the browser.

- **Price:** free, open source (MIT).
- **Sync:** No - local storage only. This is a feature, not a bug, for privacy-focused users.
- **Data:** stays in your browser's `localStorage`.
- **Permissions:** one (`tabs`), used only for "save current tab".
- **Keyboard shortcut:** `Alt+S`.
- **Best for:** people who want zero setup, total privacy, and instant access - especially on a single machine.

## Which should you use?

### Pick SaveBuddy if:
- You want links without an account or sign-up.
- You care about privacy and don't want your reading list on a server.
- You mostly use one computer.
- You want to start saving links in under a minute.

### Pick Raindrop.io if:
- You have thousands of links and want folders, tags, and screenshots.
- You switch between devices constantly.
- Full-text search is worth $28/year to you.

### Pick Pocket if:
- Your primary use is "save article to read later."
- You want a great mobile reading experience.
- You don't mind your reading list being stored on their servers.

## You don't have to pick one

Plenty of people run two tools: SaveBuddy (or bookmarks) for quick "keep this" saves, plus Pocket for articles they plan to read. The key is not to let bookmarks become a junk drawer.
